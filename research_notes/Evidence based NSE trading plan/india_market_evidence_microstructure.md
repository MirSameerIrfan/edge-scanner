## 1. What do academic and transparent practitioner studies find for intraday strategies on NSE stocks / Nifty / Bank Nifty (ORB, first→last half-hour momentum, gap fill vs continuation, intraday mean reversion, VWAP) after costs?

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous, cost-inclusive, NSE-wide study that shows a retail-executable intraday edge for ORB, gap fades, VWAP or pivot rules. The one academic NSE ORB paper (a single stock) finds results indistinguishable from chance. The one transparent VWAP backtest on Nifty and Bank Nifty futures loses money after costs. RSI-type rules failed in India even before costs. The best-supported idea is first-half-hour → last-half-hour momentum, which has one Indian academic paper behind it, but the predictive power is small and no Indian test includes costs. The base rate is poor: SEBI found 71% of individual intraday cash traders lost money in FY23, and 80% of those making more than 500 trades a year lost.

### Cited Findings
**Opening range breakout (ORB)**
- Wang & Gangwar (SSRN, 2025), "Optimizing Intraday Breakout Strategies on the NSE: A Block-Based Performance Evaluation", tested ORB variants on **Tata Motors only**. It used 5-, 15- and 30-minute opening windows, volume thresholds and several holding periods. The ORB variants showed larger cumulative returns than buy-and-hold, but **p-values were about 0.45–0.50**, and the authors conclude the evidence cannot separate the edge from chance. The summary I saw did not state cost assumptions. — [SSRN 5198458](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5198458.pdf?abstractid=5198458&mirid=1)
- Non-Indian benchmark: QuantConnect's "Opening Range Breakout for Stocks in Play" (5-minute opening range on liquid, high-relative-volume **US** stocks) reports a **Sharpe ratio of 2.4**. This is US data and not evidence for NSE. — [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)
- Indian broker and vendor ORB guides describe mechanics (15–60-minute windows, volume confirmation, stop at the opposite end of the range) but give no statistical tests. Several are screener or product pages. — [MyAlgoKart](https://malgokart.in/learning/opening-range-breakout); [OneTradeJournal ORB](https://onetradejournal.com/strategies/opening-range-breakout)

**First half-hour → last half-hour (intraday time-series momentum)**
- Indian paper "Hedging Demand and Intraday Momentum within the Indian Stock Market" reports **strong intraday momentum into the last half-hour**. The overnight-plus-first-half-hour return (ONFH) predicts better **when its sign is opposite to the prior day's return** (ROD). I saw only a partial abstract. The summary gave no cost-inclusive strategy results. — [ResearchGate 383567351](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383567351_Hedging_Demand_and_Intraday_Momentum_within_the_Indian_Stock_Market)
- A Scribd-hosted Indian study of 30-minute data over **six months** reports that last-half-hour returns depend on the prior day's return and the same day's first-half-hour return, and that momentum strategies built on these beat the index. The original paper is unverified, so the evidence is weak. — [Scribd "Intraday Momentum Trading Strategies in India"](https://www.scribd.com/document/670520471/MomentumTrading)
- US anchor study (Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, JFE 2018): S&P 500 ETF, **1993–2013**. The first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return, more strongly on high-volatility days, recession days and some macro-news days, and more again when combined with the 12th half-hour return. R² is small, and the profitability figures are gross of costs. — [ScienceDirect JFE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18301351); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)
- International evidence (Lancaster FoFI 2020 paper, "Intraday Time Series Momentum: International Evidence") finds the pattern "pervasive across countries". The rule is to go long or short in the last half-hour according to the sign of the first half-hour. I could not confirm whether India is in that sample. — [Li, FoFI 2020 PDF](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2020/files/2020/04/FoFI-2020-092-Zeming-Li.pdf)
- Practitioner cost test (QuantInsti): an intraday equity round trip costs about **0.08% (8 bps) on ₹1 lakh** at a flat-fee broker. In its Nifty 50 momentum simulation (2009–2026, "teaching illustration only"), the fast rule's **Sharpe falls to zero at about 11 bps cost**. It notes that the Nifty index cannot be traded directly, so futures or ETF roll and tracking costs come on top. — [QuantInsti, transaction costs](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/transaction-costs-automated-trading/)

**Intraday mean reversion / overreaction**
- Tripathi & Aggarwal, "Prior Return Effect in Indian Stock Market: An Intra-day Analysis" (IJFM), use high-frequency data. Returns **reverse within a few minutes** after extreme rises and falls. The effect is asymmetric: prices are pushed back up quickly after unjustified falls but come down slowly after unjustified rises. — [Publishing India IJFM](https://publishingindia.com/archive/ijfm/prior-return-effect-in-indian-stock-market-an-intra-day-analysis)
- A 2025 Journal of Asset Management paper on the Indian market, where retail intraday trading is very high, finds a **significant positive relationship between intraday overreaction and next-day returns**, which is continuation rather than reversal. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/assmgt/v26y2025i5d10.1057_s41260-025-00418-y.html)
- A 2024 Vision paper (612,066 firm-days) finds that Indian **overnight returns do not reverse** and uses them as a firm-level sentiment proxy. — [CiteDrive listing](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/make-some-noise-tonightovernight-return-as-investor-sentiment-index-indian-evidence)

**Gaps**
- I found no rigorous Indian gap-fill or gap-continuation study. A practitioner site says fill rates depend on gap size and cause. It claims large (150+ Nifty point) news-driven gaps fill only about 25% of the time on the same day, and a 78% win rate for fading small gaps, with no sample or method given. — [OneTradeJournal gap fill](https://onetradejournal.com/strategies/gap-fill-strategy)
- A TradingView "Nifty always fills the opening gap" claim rests on about one month of personal observation. — [TradingView Nifty gap observation](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/NIFTY/G7uW85qS-Nifty-Gap-Theory-Study-Observation)

**VWAP**
- Marketcalls (practitioner, transparent) tested a previous-day-VWAP intraday rule on **Nifty and Bank Nifty futures, 2011–2019**. Nifty was **profitable before commissions and slippage but not after**. Bank Nifty was **unprofitable even before costs**. — [Marketcalls VWAP test](https://www.marketcalls.in/amibroker/vwap-intraday-trading-strategy-do-simple-trading-strategies-really-work-part4.html)
- AlgoTest's VWAP option-buying test did worse than its own straddle strategy. Its case for the strategy is low correlation, not standalone returns. — [AlgoTest](https://algotest.in/blog/backtest-an-option-buying-strategy-using-vwap-template.md)

**Generic technical rules in India (academic)**
- Colombo Business Journal study (India, **Feb 2000–May 2018**, bull and bear phases): the **RSI rule failed to deliver positive returns even before transaction costs**, and RSI/MACD leave little or nothing after costs. The authors read this as support for weak-form efficiency. — [CBJ PDF](https://mgmt.cmb.ac.lk/cbj/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/2.-Technical-trading-rules.pdf); [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/79427693/Testing_the_Profitability_of_Technical_Trading_Rules_across_Market_Cycles_Evidence_from_India)
- Older work, "How rewarding is technical analysis in the Indian stock market?", found moving-average rules on Indian indices captured direction and earned significant returns long and short, and it included transaction costs. It is older, and how clear the net-of-cost results are is uncertain. — [ResearchGate 227624036](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227624036_How_rewarding_is_technical_analysis_in_the_Indian_stock_market)
- A 2026 SSRN review, "Intraday Scalping on the NSE after the 2025-26 Cost and Algorithmic-Trading Regime Changes" (Subramanya S), concludes that **transaction costs, not signal quality, usually decide whether a scalping rule survives**, and that short-horizon backtests are fragile to fill assumptions, regime change and overfitting. The search tool flagged the posting metadata as unusual, so verify before citing. — [SSRN 7547298](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7547298)

**Outcome base rates (SEBI)**
- SEBI's July 2024 study of intraday trading by individuals in the equity cash segment found that **71% made a net loss in FY23**. Among those with **more than 500 trades a year, 80%** lost. Loss-makers spent an **extra 57% of their trading losses on costs**, and profit-makers spent 19% of their profits on costs. Intraday traders grew from about 15 lakh (FY19) to 69 lakh (FY23), and the under-30 share rose from 18% to 48%. The data came from the top-10 brokers, covering about 86% of individual clients. — [SEBI report page](https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/research/jul-2024/study-analysis-of-intraday-trading-by-individuals-in-equity-cash-segment_84946.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/7-out-of-10-individual-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-segment-make-losses-sebi-study/74733.html); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-study-finds-7-10-individual-intraday-traders-equity-cash-segment-losses.html)
- SEBI F&O studies: **93% of individual F&O traders lost money over FY22–FY24** (Sept 2024 study). **About 91% lost in FY25**, with net losses of **₹1,05,603 crore** after costs, up from ₹74,812 crore in FY24. A later SEBI release reportedly revised FY25 losses to ₹1,11,788 crore and found **87.7% lost in FY26**; that later release is secondary-sourced. — [Multibagg (FY22–24)](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/sebi-fno-losses-income-data-cmt4ret9aqnj80zql88lhpocl); [Business Standard FY25](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html); [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/07/07/dcm92-biz-sebi-derivatives-study.html); [Finnovate FY26](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-fno-trader-losses-fy26-individual-derivatives)

### Inferences
- The evidence gives **no prior reason to expect ORB, gap fade, VWAP or pivot setups to be profitable after costs on NSE**. The paper-trade trial should be treated as the real test, and the null hypothesis should be "no edge".
- **Costs measured in R decide viability for this account.** The table below is my calculation from Zerodha's published intraday equity rates (see Section 6): brokerage the lower of ₹20 or 0.03% per order, STT 0.025% on the sell side, NSE transaction charge 0.00297%, SEBI fee ₹10/crore, stamp duty 0.003% on the buy side, GST 18% on brokerage plus exchange plus SEBI charges.

  | Notional per trade | Round-trip explicit cost | % of notional | Stop distance for 1R = ₹200 | Cost in R |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | ₹20,000 | ≈ ₹21.2 | 0.106% | 1.0% | ≈ 0.11R |
  | ₹50,000 | ≈ ₹53.0 | 0.106% | 0.4% | ≈ 0.27R |
  | ₹1,00,000 (5x MIS max on ₹20k) | ≈ ₹82.5 | 0.082% | 0.2% | ≈ 0.41R |

  Add slippage. With SL-M and market orders, assume at least 1 tick per side (₹0.05 on a ₹250–1,000 stock is about 0.5–2 bps per side). A tight-stop setup sized to ₹1 lakh can therefore cost about 0.5R per round trip, and it needs gross expectancy above about 0.5R per trade just to break even. Wider stops on smaller notional cut the cost in R sharply. tradingtool should record the cost in R for every paper trade.
- First-half-hour → last-half-hour momentum is the best-supported Indian intraday effect and fits a manual trader (one decision around 15:00). For F&O stocks the window was truncated between 3 Aug and 6 Dec 2026, when continuous trading ended at 15:15 and Zerodha's MIS square-off was 15:12. It returns to 15:00–15:30 from 7 Dec 2026, but Zerodha's general equity MIS square-off is 15:25 (see Section 6). It has not been tested on Indian data with costs, so it is a candidate to paper-trade, not an established edge.
- Andrew Aziz-style "stocks in play" ORB has only US backtest support (the QuantConnect replication). How well it transfers to NSE is unknown.

### Gaps
- I found no NSE-wide, multi-year, cost-inclusive academic study of ORB (5/15/30-min), gap fill/continuation, VWAP reversion or pivot rules on NSE stocks. Marketcalls and QuantInsti pages could not be opened to extract full statistics (host blocked).
- Whether the Wang & Gangwar ORB paper modelled costs is unknown.
- The Indian intraday-momentum paper's sample period, universe, R² and any cost-adjusted returns were not visible.
- No Indian study of intraday mean reversion with execution costs was found. The Tripathi & Aggarwal reversals are minutes-long and probably not capturable by hand.

## 2. Does NSE's pre-open call auction (equilibrium price, buy/sell imbalance) carry information about the opening range or the day's direction?

### Takeaway
No Indian study I found tests whether pre-open order imbalance predicts the opening range or the day's direction. The NSE pre-open literature (2010–2015 era) is about price-discovery quality and opening volatility, and its results are mixed. The pre-open mechanics were also overhauled on **7 Sept 2026**, so older studies describe a different auction.

### Cited Findings
- Acharya & Gaikwad (2014, Cogent Economics & Finance) studied **50 call-auction stocks** and found that the pre-open session (introduced **18 Oct 2010**) **did not significantly improve price discovery**: the market-model R² did not rise after its introduction. — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23322039.2014.944668)
- Jena & Dash (GARCH study): unconditional **opening volatility fell in 16 of 26 stocks** after the call auction began, which they attribute to deeper order books. — [AESS archive](https://archive.aessweb.com/index.php/5004/article/view/3849)
- Indian Journal of Finance (Nov 2015), 3 years either side of 2010: **no significant difference in returns** and **lower volatility**, and the authors conclude that price-discovery efficiency improved. This conflicts with Acharya & Gaikwad. — [IJF 81127](https://www.indianjournaloffinance.co.in/index.php/IJF/article/view/81127)
- Dinabandhu Bag's study of NSE pre-open information content, built from ticker-level order imbalance, reports that **information content improved little** over the sample and that **first-15-minute realized volatility rose** after the auction was introduced. Opening order imbalance and pre-market volume relate to realized volatility. It does not test opening returns. I could not confirm which search link hosts this paper. — [Distant Reader CJFA PDF](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/cjfa/cjfa-30366.pdf); [EconPapers RePEc:ris:ambsrv:0126](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ris:ambsrv:0126)
- Related NSE evidence, not about the pre-open: Gupta & Kumar (2025), using **2014–18 NSE limit-order-book data**, find that **deeper book levels predict short-term returns** beyond autocorrelation, volume and trade imbalance. — [arXiv 2507.22712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22712v2)
- Analogous non-Indian evidence: Chu & Qiu (China) find that **first-half-hour order imbalance** predicts returns better than daily imbalance, in- and out-of-sample. That is continuous-session imbalance, not the auction. — [vLex abstract](https://international.vlex.com/vid/forecasting-stock-returns-using-1049441872)
- **Old structure (until 6 Sept 2026):** order entry 09:00–09:08, matching 09:08–09:12, buffer 09:12–09:15. — [Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/trading-faqs/market-sessions/articles/what-are-pre-market-and-post-market-sessions-and-orders); [javajack deep dive](https://javajack.github.io/broking-kyc/deep-dives/foundational/pre-open-closing-auction/)
- **New structure (from 7 Sept 2026):** market and limit orders 09:00–09:05; **limit orders only** 09:05–09:10, with **order entry closing randomly in the last two minutes (about 09:08–09:10)**; matching 09:10–09:12; buffer 09:12–09:15. **Market orders get priority over limit orders** in matching. Pre-open trades are final. — [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/nse-pre-open-session-rules); [Business Standard 7 Sept 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/market-pre-open-rules-change-from-today-here-s-what-is-different-126090700065_1.html); [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/trading/nse-pre-open-session-new-order-rules-7-september/)
- NSE started a **pre-open session in the F&O segment from 8 Dec 2025**. NSE circular NSE/FAOP/76186 (4 Sept 2026) covers the F&O pre-open call auction go-live on 7 Sept 2026. — [Business Standard (Nov 2025)](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/capital-market-news/nse-to-commence-pre-opening-session-in-f-o-segment-from-december-08-125110400728_1.html); [NSE circular FAOP76186](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/FAOP76186.pdf)

### Inferences
- With no evidence either way, tradingtool should **log** the pre-open equilibrium price, the gap versus the previous close, and total buy versus sell quantity as features on every paper trade. It should not use them as entry rules. After a few hundred flags, the trader can check whether imbalance helps predict ORB direction.
- Under the new mechanics, the pre-open "imbalance" before the random close (about 09:08–09:10) can change at the last moment, so snapshots should be taken after 09:12 when the equilibrium price is fixed.
- The Bag result (higher first-15-minute volatility after the auction began) is a reason to be careful with very tight 5-minute ORB stops at 09:15–09:30.

### Gaps
- I found no study of whether pre-open buy/sell imbalance predicts the 09:15–09:30/09:45 range, ORB direction or the close-to-open trend on NSE.
- I found no study of the post-7-Sept-2026 auction, which is too new.
- Whether Kite Connect streams pre-open indicative equilibrium price and imbalance (and with what granularity) was not researched.

## 3. Which event and calendar effects have evidence in India (F&O expiry, quarterly results/PEAD, bulk/block deals, F&O ban entries/exits, index inclusion/exclusion, FII/DII flow days, Budget/RBI days)?

### Takeaway
The best-documented Indian event effects are **index inclusion/exclusion**, where price pressure largely reverses, and **bulk/block deals**, where pre-deal run-ups are consistent with front-running and buy deals have stronger effects. Both play out over days, not intraday, and are mostly known only after the fact. Expiry-day evidence is old and conflicting. PEAD evidence is mixed. Budget days produce large moves of unpredictable direction. I found **no rigorous study** of F&O ban entry/exit, RBI policy days, or FII flow days as intraday signals: FII flows follow returns rather than lead them.

### Cited Findings
**Current expiry calendar (rules)**
- From **20 Nov 2024**, SEBI allows each exchange weekly expiries on **one** benchmark index: NSE keeps **Nifty 50**, BSE keeps **Sensex**. — [Share.Market](https://www.share.market/buzz/insights/weekly-expiry-days-in-indian-fo-markets/)
- NSE moved Nifty weekly expiry **from Thursday to Tuesday for contracts expiring on or after 1 Sept 2025**. Monthly contracts expire on the **last Tuesday**, and BSE Sensex expires on Thursday. A May 2025 SEBI directive limits all equity-derivative expiries to Tuesday or Thursday. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/nse-receives-sebi-nod-for-tuesday-expiry-bse-thursday-480736-2025-06-17); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/why-nifty-expiry-changed-to-tuesday)
- Bank Nifty, FinNifty and other NSE indices are **monthly-only** (last Tuesday). — [OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/learn/weekly-vs-monthly-expiry); [Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/bank-nifty-weekly-expiry-change-cmua51nkj000i2znt70vvai3v)
- From **7 Dec 2026**, expiring F&O contracts settle at the **VWAP of the last 30 minutes** of regular trading, replacing the CAS closing price used from 3 Aug 2026. — [HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/sebi-revises-cas-timings-to-330-340-pm-shifts-expiring-fo-settlement-to-30-minute-vwap)

**Expiry-day effects (evidence)**
- Vipul (2005, Journal of Futures Markets): underlying share prices **dip slightly the day before expiry and rise significantly the day after**. Volume spikes on expiry day, builds the day before, and persists the day after for high-derivative-volume shares. The author attributes this to arbitrage and cash-market short-sale restrictions. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v25y2005i11p1045-1065.html)
- Bhaumik & Bose: the 2007 working paper found expiry effects on volume and on index returns and volatility. The 2009 journal version found **no effect on intraday index volatility** and lower inter-day volatility. — [Michigan DeepBlue](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/8c79ceec-dfec-4037-98e2-f10c5c56352a/full)
- Mahalwala (2016): no significant expiry effect on returns, volatility or reversal. Wats (2017): effects extend over the whole expiry week. A 2020 Journal of Asian Finance, Economics & Business study (Nifty Bank index, 2010–2020, ARMA-EGARCH) found **no volatility effect and no post-expiry reversal**. — [KoreaScience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO202032462596875.do)
- Single-stock futures cash-settled on the **last-half-hour VWAP** raise stock volatility **in the final half-hour of expiry day only** (Journal of Futures Markets paper). — [MetaTOC](https://metatoc.com/papers/19159-expiration-day-effects-and-the-impact-of-short-trading-breaks-on-intraday-volatility-evidence-from-the-indian-market)
- Bank Nifty expiries 2013–2019: after weekly options were introduced, expiry days showed "high return, low volatility and decrease in volume". — [IDEAS (IJEFI 2020)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eco/journ1/2020-03-3.html)
- A Zenodo paper reporting higher weekly-expiry volatility (stronger in Bank Nifty) uses a **simulated** dataset, so I discount it. — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/19220278)
- After Nifty weekly options launched, the negative return–India VIX correlation weakened. — [ResearchGate 363765989](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363765989_Effect_of_the_Launch_of_Nifty_Weekly_Options_on_the_Relationship_between_Nifty_and_India_VIX)

**Quarterly results / post-earnings drift**
- An NSE-listed-firms study ("Post-Earning Announcement Drift and Value-Glamour Anomalies in NSE Listed Firms") found **drift over 120 days**, with the market partly pricing surprises in before announcement. — [Trends (VUT Brno)](https://journals.vutbr.cz/index.php/trends/article/view/541)
- A study of 469 companies (2002–2011) found strong continuation patterns in earnings. — [IIMB repository](https://repository.iimb.ac.in/handle/2074/17935)
- A BSE 500 dissertation found a fairly efficient response to good news but **negative drift for over a week after bad news**. — [Nottingham eprints 26569](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/26569)
- A 2021 study of **100 NSE firms (2014–2018)** found the market **efficient** around earnings announcements, with no excess returns. — [ISBR journal PDF](https://www.isbr.in/journals/52-64.pdf)
- Mapping caveat: the search summary described these four studies and the links point to the most likely host. I did not verify that each claim matches its link exactly.

**Index inclusion/exclusion (Nifty 50)**
- Chhatwani (IIM Indore, 2000–2016): included stocks earn positive abnormal returns that **reverse within about 60 days**. Excluded stocks earn negative abnormal returns only in the first 10 days, then **about +4% to +7% abnormal returns over days 60–240**. — [Publishing India JCAR](https://publishingindia.com/archive/jcar/price-and-volume-effects-of-nifty-50-index-reorganization)
- Joshipura & Janakiramanan (2015): exclusions show a significant negative effect around announcement that does not persist. Inclusions show **no announcement effect, only a positive effective-day return that does not persist**. Effective-day volume spikes tied to index fund and ETF trading point to price pressure. — [IDEAS (AFAFA 2015)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/afasfa/v5y2015i1p21-36.html)
- Parthasarathy (1999–2010) finds **permanent** positive abnormal returns and an information explanation. Rahman & Rajib (2014) support price pressure. — [IDEAS (AJFA 2010)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/mth/ajfa88/v2y2010i2p55-80.html); [PSAU/Elsevier Pure](https://psau.sa.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/associated-effects-of-index-composition-changes-an-evidence-from-)

**Bulk/block deals**
- Rajvanshi & Paul (Managerial Finance 2022), NSE 2010–2019: front-runners can earn about **5–7% within a week** around the event day. Pre-deal abnormal returns are higher for **buy** deals. Lagged CAR, volume change and delivery change explain abnormal returns, and results hold in bull and bear periods. — [Emerald MF-08-2021-0374](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/MF-08-2021-0374/full/pdf)
- Rajvanshi (2015, Emerging Markets Review), BSE and NSE 2004–2012: **buy trades have significant positive CAR**, and effects are larger for buys than sells. — [AMiner](https://www.aminer.org/pub/56d90dd2dabfae2eee2852cf/price-manipulation-front-running-and-bulk-trades-evidence-from-india)
- Rabada & Barretto (2023), 25 Nifty Midcap Select stocks, 2012–2021: volume and delivery % **rise before bulk deals and fall sharply after**. Nifty 50 stocks had too few bulk deals to study. — [Rosary College PDF](https://rclibrary.rosarycollege.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/9JitendraHelicSandrio.pdf)
- NSE-IGIDR working paper (Ranganathan & Singh): 3-day CARs on block trades by blockholder type. Disclosure transparency after 2010 increased the price impact of blockholder trades. — [NSE-IGIDR WP4](https://archives.nseindia.com/research/content/NSE-IGIDR_CG_RPaper_2015-16_WP4.pdf)

**F&O ban (MWPL) entries/exits**
- Mechanics: a stock enters the ban when open interest exceeds **95% of MWPL** and exits at **80%**. During the ban, F&O positions can only be reduced, and intraday trading in that stock's F&O contracts is not allowed. Index F&O is unaffected. — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/knowledge-center/futures-and-options/what-is-f-and-o-ban); [Motilal Oswal](https://www.motilaloswal.com/learning-centre/2023/7/why-is-intraday-trading-not-allowed-for-contracts-if-the-underlying-security-is-under-a-ban-period); [Liquide](https://blog.liquide.life/demystifying-the-f-o-ban-its-impact-and-working-in-the-indian-stock-market)
- MWPL is now measured using **delta-based Future-Equivalent OI (FutEq)** rather than notional OI. Coverage ties the framework to **1 Oct 2025** (SEBI circular of 29 May 2025), while Zerodha dates the MWPL calculation change to **6 Dec 2025**; the dates conflict. One report says stocks enter the ban **a day after** the breach, which is meant to cut spurious bans; I found only one source for this. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/sebi-equity-fo-rules-futeq-method-mwpl-risk-management-478321-2025-05-29); [Zerodha Z-Connect (24 Dec 2025)](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/updates/understanding-the-new-delta-oi-based-mwpl-framework); [Zee Business](https://www.zeebiz.com/market-news/news-sebi-tightens-control-on-fo-trades-introduces-stricter-position-limits-and-monitoring-from-oct-1-379907)
- Price-effect claims are anecdotal and contradictory. Some brokers say bans lower volume and volatility, others say they raise volatility. One case study shows a **4% rebound on the ban-exit day** (single anecdote). — [Arihant](https://blog.arihantcapital.com/the-fo-ban-explained-impact-reasons-and-what-traders-should-know/); [Liquide](https://blog.liquide.life/demystifying-the-f-o-ban-its-impact-and-working-in-the-indian-stock-market)

**FII/DII flow days**
- Daily Granger studies find **bidirectional causality**, with FIIs mainly **chasing returns** (positive feedback). Heavy FII trading moves returns only very briefly. — [Emerald (2012)](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/17554191211274794/full/html); [MPRA 15793](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/15793/1/MPRA_paper_15793.pdf); [IUP IJAF](https://www.iupindia.in/305/IJAF_Foreign_Institutional_Investment_16.html)
- A 2025 study (daily data 2017–2022) finds bidirectional Nifty 50–FII causality after COVID, a shift from before. — [Business Perspectives (IMFI)](https://businessperspectives.org/journals/investment-management-and-financial-innovations/issue-493/domestic-institutional-investors-integration-with-nifty-50-in-the-indian-equity-market)

**Union Budget / RBI days**
- Budget days 2001–2025: Nifty **fell on 15 and rose on 10**, with an average absolute move of about 0.19%, but **moves larger than 1% occurred 16 times**. — [Business Today (1 Feb 2026)](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/budget-2026-stock-market-strategy-why-traders-need-to-be-extra-cautious-avoid-noise-today-513839-2026-02-01)
- Bank Nifty implied volatility rose to **43.4% the day before a Budget and fell to 30.5% at the Budget-day close** (Upstox analysis; year unspecified). Nifty rose on only 11 of 24 Budget days. — [Upstox (options into Budget)](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/trading/budget-2025-how-to-trade-options-leading-up-to-the-budget-day/article-142620); [Upstox (index reactions)](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/trading/how-do-major-indices-react-to-union-budget/article-65567)
- Budget 2026 (1 Feb 2026): indices fell **nearly 2%** on the STT hike announcement. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/markets/union-budget-2026-stt-on-futures-hiked-by-150-options-by-50-markets-tank-3882160)
- An arXiv event study (2025) of the 2024 Budget on Nifty 50 (AAR/CAAR, −15 to +15 days, compared with 2020/2022/2023). — [IDEAS/arXiv 2502.15787](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2502.15787.html)

### Inferences
- **For an intraday-only plan, none of these events is a proven entry signal.** Their practical use is as **risk filters** and as "in play" catalysts: results days, bulk/block-deal names and index-change names draw volume, which is what an in-play scanner looks for.
- Index inclusion/exclusion and bulk-deal effects are **multi-day (swing) phenomena**. The front-running pattern means much of the move comes before public disclosure, so a retail trader acting on published deal data is late.
- Expiry: the most consistent Indian mechanism is **last-half-hour volatility on expiry days when settlement uses the last-30-minute VWAP**, and that rule returns from 7 Dec 2026. A reasonable rule is to avoid opening new late-day positions in F&O stocks on monthly expiry (last Tuesday), or at least tag such flags in paper trading.
- Budget days (and probably RBI days, though untested) carry large, direction-unpredictable moves. Tag them as "event days" and consider skipping or halving size, because one gap-through-stop can cost several R.
- F&O-ban stocks can still be traded intraday in the cash market. Because no study exists, ban entry and exit should be logged as features and not traded as signals.

### Gaps
- I found no academic event study of F&O ban entry or exit returns, of RBI MPC policy days (intraday Nifty/Bank Nifty), or of FII/DII provisional-flow announcements as next-day predictors.
- I found no Indian study of **intraday** behaviour on results day (opening drive versus fade), which is the scenario a day trader actually faces.
- I found no Indian expiry-effect study after the move to Tuesday expiry or after CAS.
- When NSE publishes bulk/block deal data, and the current block-deal window rules, were not verified this session.

## 4. How do NSE price bands (2/5/10/20% fixed for non-F&O; dynamic for F&O) affect breakouts, stops and gap trades? Is there a circuit "magnet" effect?

### Takeaway
Non-F&O stocks have **fixed daily bands of 2%, 5%, 10% or 20%**. The tighter bands are applied as a **surveillance action**. F&O stocks have **no fixed band** but a **10% dynamic operating range** that widens in steps (5% / 3% / 2%) after cooling-off periods (sliding mechanism live since **18 Nov 2024**). I found **no rigorous study of a circuit magnet effect for NSE single stocks**. The international evidence is mixed. For a hand-trading intraday account, the practical risk of fixed bands is **stops that cannot execute** when a stock locks at its circuit.

### Cited Findings
- NSE: daily price bands of **2%, 5% or 10%** apply to specified securities, **no bands for securities with derivatives** (which keep a **10% daily operating range**), and **20%** for all remaining scrips. Bands are set from the previous close. — [NSE equity market price bands](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-market-price-bands)
- Tighter bands are a **surveillance tool**. Exchanges revise securities to 10% and 5% bands as surveillance actions, and NSE's periodic "changes in price bands" circulars move securities between bands, often tied to enhanced surveillance or volatility. — [BSE surveillance page](https://www.bseindia.com/static/markets/equity/EQReports/sur_Surveillance.html); [NSE price-band change circular (Feb 2026, mirror)](https://rhnvrm.github.io/stock-market-circulars/circulars/nse/2026/nse-2026-02-04-db6bd23472481d0c-changes-in-price-bands-from-february-05-2026); [NSE price-band change circular (Apr 2026, mirror)](https://rhnvrm.github.io/stock-market-circulars/circulars/nse/2026/nse-2026-04-07-b7f93c9c96e0244b-changes-in-price-bands-from-april-08-2026)
- **F&O stocks, sliding dynamic band (live 18 Nov 2024):** initial band 10%. **First two flexes: +5% of previous close each**, after a 15-minute cooling-off, or 5 minutes if triggered in the last half-hour. **Next two flexes: +3% each** after 30-minute cooling-off. **Subsequent flexes: +2%** after 60-minute cooling-off. — [Fyers notice](https://fyers.in/notice-board/revised-dynamic-price-bands-and-fo-segment-rules/); [Zerodha explainer](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explainer-on-new-price-band-rules-for-stocks-trading-in-the-fo-segment); [Navia](https://naviamarkets.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/1000326846-price-band-rules-in-f-o-segment); [NSE circular CMTR64994](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMTR64994.pdf); [NSE circular FAOP64995](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/FAOP64995.pdf)
- SEBI has reportedly considered (Apr 2026) **uniform dynamic bands for F&O stocks across exchanges**. A newly added F&O stock gets dynamic bands on NSE while BSE may keep a static 20% cap. I could not confirm whether this was finalized. — [Pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/legal/news/sebi-weighs-uniform-price-bands-for-fo-stocks-across-exchanges-to-fix-mismatch-12280104-939f-4d75-869d-5852c9b0784c/); [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/sebi-to-enforce-dynamic-price-bands-for-all-fo-stocks-equally-on-exchanges)
- Index-level market-wide circuit breakers (on Nifty 50/Sensex) halt all trading on the exchange. They are separate from stock bands. — [OneTradeJournal upper circuit](https://onetradejournal.com/glossary/upper-circuit)
- Example of how often circuits are hit: on one June 2025 morning, **42 stocks hit upper bands and 18 hit lower bands**, concentrated in small/mid-cap and SME names. — [HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/over-40-stocks-hit-upper-price-band-as-market-momentum-accelerates)
- **Magnet effect, general literature:** contested. Some studies (e.g., Chinese markets) find returns accelerating near limits. Others find it weak or absent. The March 2020 global circuit-breaker events show accelerating returns and falling volume before limit hits. A survey lists NSE thresholds of 10/15/20% with time-of-day-dependent halt durations. Those thresholds match NSE's index-level market-wide breaker, although the search summary called them stock-level bands. The same survey notes evidence that large investors push prices to the upper limit and sell the next day. — [Sifat & Mohamad 2020 survey (IIUM)](https://irep.iium.edu.my/84726/1/Sifat%20Mohamad%202020%20IREF.pdf); [Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X19305128); [EFMA 2019](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2019-Azores/papers/EFMA2019_0371_fullpaper.pdf); [Hawaii (2009)](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~rheesg/Published%20Papers/2009/An%20Analysis%20of%20the%20Magnet%20Effect.pdf)
- Broker and education sites say hitting a circuit does not predict later performance. These are opinion, not data. — [Upstox help](https://help.upstox.com/support/solutions/articles/41535-what-are-price-bands-and-circuit-breakers-)

### Inferences
- **Restrict the watchlist to F&O-list stocks.** They have no fixed circuit, only a 10% operating range that flexes. A breakout or a stop near the band pauses during cooling-off but is not locked for the day. Non-F&O names in 2% or 5% bands can lock limit-up or limit-down. A locked stock means (a) a stop cannot fill, (b) MIS positions may not be squared off, and (c) the ORB range is capped mechanically, so a "breakout" can just be a run to the band.
- For F&O stocks, a move to ±10% leads to a cooling-off of at least 15 minutes during which orders outside the band are rejected. A short entered near +9.8% cannot be stopped out above +10% until the band flexes, so stops placed near a band carry extra gap risk.
- With no NSE evidence of a magnet effect, "circuit chase" setups (buying stocks approaching the upper band) have no support. The concentration of circuit hits in SME and small caps makes them a poor fit for a ₹200-risk, hand-executed account.
- tradingtool should store each instrument's band (fixed 2/5/10/20, or dynamic) and its distance to the band, and drop any flag whose stop or target lies beyond the current band.

### Gaps
- I found no NSE single-stock study of returns or volatility as prices approach fixed bands (magnet test), or of next-day returns after upper or lower circuit hits. A thesis-style Nifty 50 market-wide circuit-breaker study reporting a "weak magnet, comparable repellent effect" appeared in search summaries, but its URL could not be identified.
- The exact conditions that trigger an F&O band flex (e.g., futures trades at the band) were not extracted from the NSE circulars.
- NSE's criteria for placing a security in the 2% or 5% band (surveillance formulas) were not found.

## 5. Liquidity: how many NSE stocks can take ₹20,000–₹1,00,000 intraday trades with negligible impact? Typical spreads and impact by segment; implications for a 15–20 stock watchlist and a daily "in play" filter

### Takeaway
At ₹20k–₹1L per order, **market impact is negligible for essentially all of the ~213–216 F&O-list stocks**. Each must clear SEBI's F&O liquidity thresholds (median quarter-sigma order size ≥ ₹75 lakh, average daily delivery value ≥ ₹35 crore), and Nifty 50 stocks must have impact cost ≤ 0.50% on far larger orders. The binding costs at this size are **brokerage, STT and the bid-ask spread/slippage**, not impact. Midcap and smallcap spreads widen sharply (blog estimates only: about 0.05–0.2% for midcaps, about 0.5–5% for thin small and micro caps). I found no authoritative NSE table of spreads by segment.

### Cited Findings
- NSE treats **impact cost** as its preferred liquidity measure. To be eligible for Nifty 50, a stock must reliably show **impact cost < 0.50% on Nifty 50 trades of ₹2 crore**. An older NSE methodology version states the 0.50% test for **90% of observations over six months on a ₹10 crore portfolio**, so the sources disagree on basket size. — [NSE indices FAQ](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/indices-faqs); [NiftyIndices impact cost](https://www.niftyindices.com/resources/index-concepts/impact-cost); [Methodology document (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/661775763/Method-NIFTY-Equity-Indices); [Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/nifty-futures/)
- An NSE release (older, at the Nifty Midcap 150 launch) compared trading value of about **₹752 crore/day for Nifty 100 vs about ₹44 crore/day for Nifty Smallcap 250**. Whether these are index totals or per-stock figures is unclear from the snippet. — [NSE Midcap 150 release](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NSE_Midcap_150_Final.pdf)
- Spread estimates (blogs, **low confidence**, inconsistent): midcaps about **0.05–0.2%**, thin small and micro caps often **0.5–5%**. Another blog gives absolute spreads of ₹0.05–0.50 for Nifty 50, ₹0.50–2.00 for Midcap 150, and ₹2–10+ for small caps. — [OneTradeJournal bid-ask](https://onetradejournal.com/glossary/bid-ask-spread); [MyChores](https://mychores.in/bid-ask-spread-in-indian-stock-trading-a-beginner-s-guide-to-market-mechanics)

- **SEBI F&O eligibility (June 2024 framework):** a stock must be in the **top 500 by average market cap and traded value (6-month rolling)**, with **MQSOS ≥ ₹75 lakh**, **MWPL ≥ ₹1,500 crore**, and **average daily delivery value (ADDV) ≥ ₹35 crore**. One Angel One page states "₹35 lakh", which conflicts. — [Business Standard (27 Jun 2024)](https://business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-tweaks-criteria-for-inclusion-deletion-of-stocks-from-f-o-segment-124062701130_1.html); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/retirement/news/sebi-makes-entry-and-exit-into-fo-stricter-to-boost-market-stability-check-details); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/knowledge-center/futures-and-options/all-you-need-to-know-about-the-inclusion-of-stocks-indices-in-the-fo-segment)

- **Turnover concentration:** in Jan 2026 the **top-10 securities' share of NSE cash turnover rose to 18.4% from 11.0%**, and 67% of the incremental turnover came from those names. I could not confirm which of these two linked pages carries the figure. In 2021 the top-100 share was reported to have fallen from 87% (May 2020) to about 50%. May 2026 cash turnover hit a 22-month high, driven by mid and small caps. — [5paisa (Jan 2026)](https://www.5paisa.com/news/equity-cash-turnover-hits-16-month-high-of-rs239-lakh-crore-in-january-2026); [Informist](https://informistmedia.com/MoneyWire/34757/Trading-Dynamics-NSE-says-trading-dynamic-shifting-in-equity-cash-large-investors-moderating); [Business Standard (2021)](https://business-standard.com/article/markets/top-100-companies-share-in-cash-market-turnover-drops-over-the-last-year-121060800054_1.html); [Angel One (May 2026)](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/cash-market-turnover-hits-22-month-high-at-1-52-trillion-in-may-2026-mid-and-small-cap-stocks-drive-surge-derivatives-subdued)
- **Intraday leverage cap:** since SEBI's peak-margin phase 4 (Sept 2021), the minimum equity-intraday margin is **20% of trade value (5x leverage)**, and F&O intraday needs 100% of NRML margin (1x). The phase-3 interim level was 15% (6.66x). ### Inferences
- **How many stocks qualify:** with MQSOS ≥ ₹75 lakh, a ₹1 lakh order is about 1/75 of the order size that SEBI's metric links to a quarter-sigma price move. That uses the standard SEBI definition of MQSOS (median quarter-sigma order size), which I did not re-fetch here. So **all ~213–216 F&O stocks are effectively "zero-impact" at this account's size**. Nifty 50 and Nifty Next 50 names are the safest subset. Many non-F&O midcaps can absorb ₹1 lakh too, but they face fixed circuits (Section 4) and wider spreads.
- **Size ceiling:** ₹20,000 capital × 5x MIS gives at most about **₹1,00,000 notional**, which is exactly the trader's stated range. With 1R = ₹200, that means a **minimum stop distance of about 0.2%**. Below 0.2% the position would need more than 5x leverage. Tight stops near 0.2% also make costs about 0.4–0.5R per trade (see the Section 1 table).
- **Watchlist construction (15–20 names):** draw from the F&O list (preferably Nifty 50, Nifty Next 50 and the most liquid F&O midcaps). Prefer prices of ₹250 or more so the tick (₹0.05) is 2 bps or less, but also avoid very high-priced stocks where one share is a large part of ₹1 lakh. Exclude names in or entering the F&O ban, names under ASM/GSM, and names with a quoted spread above about 0.05% at the time of the flag.
- **Daily "in play" filter:** evidence-informed candidates, none validated on NSE, are: (i) relative volume in the first 15–30 minutes versus the same window's 20-day average; (ii) gap versus 14-day ATR; (iii) a scheduled catalyst (results, index change, bulk/block deal reported the previous evening); (iv) sector or index alignment. Log each as a feature so the paper trades can show which matter.
- Rising turnover concentration in the top-10 names (Jan 2026) means the "in play" set on a given day is often small. A 15–20 name watchlist is reasonable for manual monitoring.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative NSE Market Pulse or academic table of average **quoted or effective spreads (bps)** by segment (Nifty 50 / Next 50 / F&O / Midcap 150 / Smallcap 250) for 2025–26.
- The current Nifty Midcap 150 and Smallcap 250 basket impact costs from the NiftyIndices factsheets could not be retrieved.
- The exact count of non-F&O stocks with ADDV above a given threshold (e.g., ₹10 crore) was not found.
- No Indian evidence was found on which "in play" metrics predict intraday follow-through.

## 6. Current session timings, closing-price method, tick sizes (2025–26 changes), intraday short-selling rules, MIS auto square-off, costs (as of 31 Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
2025–26 brought many changes, and another lands on **7 Dec 2026**. Since **3 Aug 2026**, F&O-list stocks stop continuous trading at **15:15**, followed by a **closing auction session (CAS) to 15:35**, and equity derivatives trade to **15:40**. Non-F&O stocks still trade to **15:30** with a **30-minute VWAP close**. From **7 Dec 2026**, all stocks trade continuously to **15:30**, CAS for F&O stocks moves to **15:30–15:40**, and expiry settlement returns to the last-30-minute VWAP. The pre-open was restructured on **7 Sept 2026**. Tick sizes were revised on **15 Apr 2025** and are now reviewed monthly. Zerodha's MIS square-off is **15:25** for equity, **15:26** for F&O, and **15:12** for CAS (F&O-list) stocks. Retail shorting is allowed intraday, but naked shorting is banned.

## 7. What setups and rules does Gujral & Vaidya's "How to Make Money in Intraday Trading" contain, and does any study test them?

### Takeaway
The book (Vision Books, 2018) is a practitioner manual built around the **"3Ms" (method, money management, mind-set)** and three **technical pillars: moving averages (20-period main MA), pivots (floor pivots and the Central Pivot Range), and "exceptional" candles**. It covers how to trade the opening and "morning range", trends (four entry tactics, candle-based stops, MA trailing stops, pyramiding), counter-trend trades, gaps, sideways markets, confluence and news catalysts. It illustrates these with 200+ chart examples. **I found no study, academic or practitioner, that tests the book's rules.** Reviewers describe it as basic and suited to beginners.

## 8. Popular Indian intraday methods (CPR, Camarilla pivots, Supertrend, 15-minute ORB, VWAP): are there rigorous tests?

### Takeaway
None of these methods has a rigorous, cost-inclusive, out-of-sample test on NSE stocks that I could find. **CPR and Camarilla** have no published Nifty statistics, only definitions and untested rules of thumb. **Supertrend** has a few practitioner backtests on Nifty and Bank Nifty futures with unclear or garbled cost handling, plus one warning about look-ahead bias. **15-minute ORB** has only anecdotal TradingView posts and a single-stock academic paper that finds no significance. **VWAP** has one transparent practitioner test that fails after costs.

### Cited Findings
- **CPR definition:** pivot P = (H+L+C)/3 and bottom central level BC = (H+L)/2, from the previous period's H/L/C. TC mirrors BC on the other side of P. Guides claim that a **wide CPR means a sideways or low-volatility day** and that a **narrow CPR (under about 0.3% of price)** precedes directional breaks above TC or below BC. Neither claim is tested on Nifty. — [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/central-pivot-range.md); [Jainam](https://www.jainam.in/?p=19465)
- A Nifty pivot calculator claims "R2 & S2 represent the 80% probability boundary of the day's range" **without data or method**. A Camarilla rule of thumb holds that Nifty staying between H3 and L3 means a range day and that a break of H4/L4 means an explosive breakout (untested). The only Camarilla statistics found (touch and continuation rates) come from **2,482 days of Nasdaq (NQ) futures**, not Nifty. — [StockCalc](https://stockcalc.in/calculator/nifty-technical-levels); [TradingView Camarilla scripts](https://tw.tradingview.com/scripts/camarilla/)
- **Supertrend:**
  - Marketcalls' Nifty-futures Supertrend backtests (2012 series) assumed **₹100/leg brokerage** and **next-bar-open execution**. Forum comments cite "1400% without brokerage" results measured "arrow to arrow", which suggests headline figures exclude costs. — [Marketcalls Supertrend backtest](https://www.marketcalls.in/amibroker/how-to-backtest-supertrend-strategy-for-nifty-futures-using-amibroker.html); [Marketcalls July 2012 report](https://www.marketcalls.in/amibroker/supertrend-backtest-report-for-july-nifty-future-series-from-29th-june-2012-till-date.html)
  - Matt Radtke's PDF (2019) claims Nifty and Bank Nifty can be traded profitably with an intraday Supertrend strategy. "Version 1" shows about **24.87% annual return** with **−18.55% max drawdown**, but the transaction-cost line is unreadable in the extract. — [Radtke PDF (pdfcoffee)](https://pdfcoffee.com/download/beating-the-market-with-a-simple-supertrend-strategy-matt-radtke-march-14-2019-pdf-free.html)
  - Share.Market's "200,000 trades" Supertrend test on **Nifty 500 stocks, 2012–2025**: no figures were visible in the snippet. — [Share.Market](https://www.share.market/buzz/insights/i-backtested-200000-trades-using-the-supertrend-indicator-here-is-what-actually-works)
  - A Scribd project on Nifty Bank Supertrend 2007–2023 gave no returns or costs in the snippet. — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/841291693/Project)
  - A practitioner momentum-burst test on Nifty and Bank Nifty futures (not Supertrend) found that **results collapsed once look-ahead bias was fixed**. — [Mohit Sharma blog](https://mohit-sharma-website.pages.dev/blogs/nifty-banknifty-momentum-burst-strategy)
- **15-minute ORB:**
  - A TradingView "1st 15-minute range breakout" post (Bank Nifty) with an RSI/EMA filter gives one worked example and no statistics. — [TradingView](https://id.tradingview.com/chart/BANKNIFTY/LAQD8uz0-1st-15-minute-range-breakout-system-for-day-trading)
  - Marketcalls' "Full Gap Open Range" Bank Nifty strategy (5-minute chart, 3 years of data, ₹100/leg, 2.5% target) did not show its report figures in the indexed text. — [Marketcalls](https://www.marketcalls.in/?p=31235)
  - The only academic NSE ORB paper (Wang & Gangwar) is single-stock with p ≈ 0.45–0.50 (Section 1). — [SSRN 5198458](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5198458.pdf?abstractid=5198458&mirid=1)
- **VWAP:** Marketcalls' previous-day-VWAP rule on Nifty futures (2011–2019) was profitable gross but not net of commissions and slippage. On Bank Nifty it was unprofitable even gross. — [Marketcalls](https://www.marketcalls.in/amibroker/vwap-intraday-trading-strategy-do-simple-trading-strategies-really-work-part4.html)

### Inferences
- The popularity of these methods in India (CPR, Camarilla, Supertrend, 15-min ORB, VWAP) **is not evidence that they work**. The few transparent tests point to edges that are gross-only or fragile (VWAP fails after costs, momentum collapses after a bias fix). Because CPR is also a core Gujral & Vaidya chapter, tradingtool's paper trades will produce India-specific evidence that does not otherwise exist.
- For honest self-testing, each method needs:
  - fixed, pre-registered rules;
  - entries at the next bar or tick after the signal, not the signal bar's close;
  - the Section 1 cost model plus at least 1 tick of slippage per side;
  - forced exits at the broker square-off times in Section 6;
  - results reported in R, with the 10R drawdown stop as the decision rule.
- The ₹20k account's 10R stop budget is small relative to the variance of a low-win-rate breakout method. A trial may stop before enough trades accrue to separate skill from luck. Paper-trading every flag in parallel (as tradingtool does) is the right way to collect sample size without spending the live drawdown budget.

### Gaps
- I found no published CPR, Camarilla or floor-pivot backtest on Nifty, Bank Nifty or NSE stocks with a stated sample, method and costs.
- Marketcalls and QuantInsti full reports (Supertrend, ORB, VWAP) could not be opened to extract win rates, expectancy and drawdowns (host blocked).
- I found no Zerodha Varsity quantitative test of these methods; Varsity content appears to be educational rather than backtest-based.
- No study compares 15-minute vs 5-minute vs 30-minute opening ranges across a broad NSE universe after costs.
