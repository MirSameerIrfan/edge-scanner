# Swing Slowly, Test Hard, Spend Little

For a ₹20,000 Zerodha account risking ₹200 a trade, the best-evidenced way to use NSE is **long-only swing trading of liquid F&O-list stocks bought for delivery (CNC), flagged from daily candles by the signals with Indian evidence behind them (6–12-month momentum and nearness to the 52-week high, under a market-trend filter) and held for weeks**, with the Gujral & Vaidya and Aziz intraday setups kept on paper until they pass the same tests, and F&O dropped. Swing is the only mode where charges at ₹200 risk (**0.10–0.15R per round trip at 3–8% stops**) sit well below a plausible edge, against 0.21R for intraday at a 0.5% stop before slippage and 0.31R for a bought Nifty option whose ₹200 stop is just 3.08 premium points; it also avoids the groups SEBI found losing most, **71% of intraday traders in FY23 (80% of those making 500+ trades a year) and 87.7% of F&O traders in FY26**. No profit is assured and no regulator has measured swing traders' results: the best-documented premium, the Nifty200 Momentum 30 index's 18.8% against 13.9% CAGR since 2005, came with a 70% drawdown in 2007–08. Even a genuine +0.2R edge at 6–12 trades a month earns only ₹240–480 a month, **less than the tool's fixed costs as specified (Kite Connect at ₹500 plus Claude Pro at about ₹1,760, roughly 11% of the account every month)**, so the ₹20,000 phase is a cheap way to measure whether an edge exists, and it pays only if a proven edge is later traded with more capital or a leaner stack. Proof takes far more than 100 trades: replay frozen rules over 10+ years of daily history with every variant counted, then paper-trade every flag under a sequential test (at a per-trade spread of 1.25R, pass when cumulative P&L reaches ₹4,332 plus ₹20 per trade, fail at −₹2,435 plus ₹20 per trade), and measure the ₹2,000 stop from the starting balance rather than the peak. tradingtool should never place orders, which keeps it outside SEBI's retail-algo rules, and should log in to Kite by hand each day and run one deterministic code path for replay and live. Claude's verdicts can only be scored forward, because its training data runs to June 2026, and Claude Pro's consumer terms bar relying on the service to buy or sell securities.

## Costs in R and base rates rule out F&O and tight intraday stops

Every large study of Indian retail traders points the same way. SEBI's study of individual intraday traders in the equity cash segment, released on 24 July 2024, found that **71% made net losses in FY23**, up from 65% in FY19. The share reached **80% among those making more than 500 trades a year**, and loss-makers paid trading costs equal to a further 57% of their trading losses ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2024/1721818140715.pdf); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)). Derivatives are worse. **87.7% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY26** (90.9% in FY25), and options produced 92% of individual losses. Costs turned about 4.4 lakh traders who were profitable before costs into net losers, and only **0.5% of traders active in every year from FY22 to FY26 were profitable in all five** ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf); [K G Somani](https://www.kgsomani.com/blog/sebi-study-877-of-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-despite-falling-participation/); [CoinSwitch summary](https://coinswitch.co/switch/personal-finance/sebi-profitability-report/); [EdgeX](https://pro.edgex.exchange/en-US/news/article/sebi-study-fo-traders-lost-money-fy26)). Abroad, fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders could "predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees" ([Barber et al. 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418113000190)). In Brazil, 97% of futures day traders who kept going beyond 300 days lost money, with no sign of learning ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://ideas.repec.org/p/spa/wpaper/2019wpecon47.html)). No regulator publishes outcomes for delivery swing traders, so the case for swing rests on indirect evidence. The traits that SEBI and Zerodha link with better results all point away from fast intraday trading and option buying: trading less often, being older, using futures or cash shares rather than bought options, and having some cash-market activity ([mostlyeconomics summary of SEBI's behaviour study](https://mostlyeconomics.wordpress.com/2026/08/25/trading-behaviour-of-individual-traders-in-the-equity-derivatives-segment-fy25-fy26/); [Kamath](https://nithinkamath.substack.com/p/unintended-consequences-of-higher)).

Costs confirm what the base rates suggest, because a fixed ₹200 risk turns every percentage charge into a fraction of R. Position size is ₹200 divided by the stop distance, so **cost in R equals the round-trip cost percentage divided by the stop percentage**. Zerodha's charges are:

| Charge | Intraday (MIS) | Delivery (CNC) |
|---|---|---|
| Brokerage | lower of 0.03% or ₹20 per order | nil |
| STT | 0.025% on the sell | 0.1% on each side |
| NSE transaction charge (all-in since 1 March 2026) | ₹307 per crore | ₹307 per crore |
| Depository (DP) charge | none | ₹15.34 per scrip on each sale |

Sources: [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/); [Zerodha STT](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/how-is-the-securities-transaction-tax-stt-calculated); [NSE FA73061](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/FA73061.pdf); [Zerodha on X](https://x.com/zerodhaonline/status/1909141317135085972).

After stamp duty, SEBI's fee and GST, an intraday round trip costs **about 0.106% of notional** and a delivery round trip **about 0.222% plus the DP charge**. A 0.5% intraday stop therefore costs 0.21R before slippage, and a 0.3% stop costs 0.35R. A 5% swing stop costs 0.12R, about two-thirds of it the fixed DP charge.

F&O fails on two counts. The Budget raised STT from 1 April 2026 to 0.05% on futures and 0.15% on option premium ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/markets-fm-hikes-stt-on-fo-trades-surprise-move-126020100888_1.html)). One Nifty lot is 65 units ([HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/nse-revises-market-lot-sizes-for-major-index-derivatives-effective-january-2026)), about ₹15 lakh of exposure at the 30 October close of 23,053 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/stock-market-live-bse-sensex-today-nifty50-gift-nifty-crude-oil-prices-ipo-today-q2-results-october-30-126103000074_1.html)). ₹200 of risk is therefore a **3.08-point stop**, well inside a minute of normal price noise. Futures or option-selling margin of roughly ₹1.6–2.4 lakh is **8–12 times the account** ([AlgoTest](https://algotest.in/blog/nifty-lot-size/)). Buying one lot of a ₹100 option costs ₹62.61 per round trip, or 0.31R, and each point of slippage adds another 0.33R.

The break-even win rates below show what those costs demand. They are computed from the rates cited above; without costs, break-even is 50% at a 1:1 reward-to-risk ratio and 33.3% at 1:2. The slippage column assumes a 0.10% round trip for shares (a Nifty 50 stock entered 15–30 seconds after a flag) and one premium point for the option. These slippage figures are assumptions, because no Indian study measures how much manual orders slip.

| Intraday, 1% stop | 0.11R | 55.3% / 36.9% | 60.3% / 40.2% |
| Swing CNC, 5% stop | 0.12R | 56.1% / 37.4% | 57.1% / 38.0% |

With no edge at all, charges alone would use up the 10R trial budget in about 47 intraday trades at a 0.5% stop (about 24 with slippage), or about 83 swing trades at a 5% stop.

Slippage also grows as the stop tightens. A hand-placed order entered 15–60 seconds after a flag might drift about 0.04–0.10% on a large-cap and roughly twice that on a volatile midcap. The drift is several times larger in the first minutes after 9:15, when spreads are widest. These are model estimates built from NSE tick sizes and its U-shaped pattern of intraday liquidity, not measurements ([Monash-hosted NSE liquidity study](https://www.monash.edu/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/925811/intraday_liquidity_patterns_in_indian_stock_market.pdf); [Fyers tick-size notice](https://fyers.in/notice-board/tick-size-revision-for-nse-derivatives-cash-segment-effective-april-15-2025/)). The same 0.05% fill error costs 0.2R on a 0.5% stop but only 0.02R on a 5% stop. That is also why backtests on daily bars are far more trustworthy than backtests on minute bars at this account size. Kite serves daily candles in requests of up to 2,000 days, but minute candles only 60 days at a time, back to about 2015. It has **no history for expired options**, so an options strategy cannot be backtested from Kite at all ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/16124/historical-15-minute-ohlc-data-availability-pricing-and-api-limits-for-equity-stocks); [Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/14806/historical-data-is-now-free); [Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/14149/historical-data-retention-policy)).

|---|---|---|---|

| Charges per trade at ₹200 risk | 0.21R at a 0.5% stop; 0.11R at 1% | 0.10–0.15R at 3–8% stops | 0.25–0.31R, plus 0.33R per point of slippage |
| Fits ₹20,000? | yes, but stops tighter than 0.2% would need more than 5× margin | yes, without leverage; about five positions at 5% stops | futures and option selling no; option buying only with 3-point stops |
| Monthly charges at typical frequency | ₹850–1,700 (20–40 trades) | ₹145–290 (6–12 trades) | about ₹1,250 (20 trades) |

The trader's ability to watch the whole session does not change this ranking. Screen time makes intraday possible, but it ties the trader to a mode whose costs match or exceed every edge that independent replications have found. The session is better used to paper-trade intraday flags and to log the real delay between a flag and a click, which no Indian study has measured.

## Momentum near 52-week highs is the only edge with Indian evidence

The strongest Indian evidence is for holding periods of one to six months. NSE's Nifty200 Momentum 30 index picks 30 stocks by their 6- and 12-month returns, adjusted for volatility, and rebalances twice a year. It returned **18.8% CAGR from April 2005 to June 2026, against 13.9% for the Nifty 200 TRI** ([HDFC Mutual Fund](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf); [NSE Indices whitepaper](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/nifty200_momentum_30_index_whitepaper_sep_20.pdf)). The cost was higher volatility, about 20.5% a year against 13.2% in NSE's own backtest ([NSE Indices whitepaper](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/nifty200_momentum_30_index_whitepaper_sep_20.pdf)), and severe crashes. One independent backtest puts the 2007–08 drawdown at **−70.5%, with a 65-month recovery**, and the index lost about 2.5% in the year to June 2026 ([BacktestIndia](https://backtestindia.com/blog/momentum-investing-india-backtest); [HDFC Mutual Fund](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf)).

Academic studies agree the momentum effect exists in India but disagree on whether it survives costs. Momentum was profitable on NSE over 1997–2013 ([Maheshwari & Dhankar](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2785526)), while other work found much of it disappears after transaction costs ([Publishing India](https://www.publishingindia.com/storage/PDFBrochures/2192.pdf)). A non-peer-reviewed NSE backtest for 2004–2024 that includes delisted stocks, and is net of 25 bps a side plus capital-gains tax, reports a "real but modest" edge that depends on a market moving-average filter ([GitHub](https://github.com/bHandreaPeR/nse-momentum-research)). The **52-week-high effect** (buying stocks trading near their highest price of the past year) has one large recent study covering more than 4,400 Indian stocks from October 2004 to August 2023. It finds the effect distinct from standard momentum, with steadier excess returns and weaker long-term reversal ([Quantpedia summary of Raju](https://quantpedia.com/an-analysis-of-52-weeks-high-effect-on-indian-stocks/)). In US data, momentum kept the largest excess return after costs among the market anomalies tested ([Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20721/w20721.pdf)).

Little else survives. Moving-average rules on Indian indices earned significant returns before costs, but bid-ask spreads and brokerage erased them ([Mitra 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v11y2011i2p287-297.html)). An RSI rule lost money on the Sensex over 2000–2018 even before costs ([Colombo Business Journal](https://mgmt.cmb.ac.lk/cbj/index.php/testing-the-profitability-of-technical-trading-rules-across-market-cycles-evidence-from-india/)). Evidence on drift after earnings announcements is contested. An IIM Bangalore study finds drift in NSE stocks ([IIMB](https://repository.iimb.ac.in/handle/2074/17935)), while a 2014–2018 study of 100 NSE firms finds none ([ISBR](https://www.isbr.in/journals/52-64.pdf)). The clearest result is that prices keep falling for more than a week after bad news ([Nottingham](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/26569)). Profiting from that would require shorting overnight, which a delivery account cannot do because naked short selling is banned ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-allows-short-selling-by-all-investors-ban-on-naked-short-selling-continues/73065.html)). It is therefore useful only as a reason to skip long trades after poor results. Gains after a stock joins the Nifty 50 reverse within about 60 days ([Chhatwani](https://publishingindia.com/archive/jcar/price-and-volume-effects-of-nifty-50-index-reorganization)). Most gains around bulk deals go to front-runners before the deals are disclosed ([Rajvanshi & Paul](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/MF-08-2021-0374/full/pdf)). Neither suits a trader acting on published data.

The intraday evidence is thinner still. The only academic test of opening-range breakouts (ORB) on NSE covers a single stock, Tata Motors, and finds p-values of 0.45–0.50, no better than chance ([Wang & Gangwar](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5198458.pdf?abstractid=5198458&mirid=1)). A transparent test of a VWAP rule on Nifty futures was profitable before costs but not after, and on Bank Nifty it lost money even before costs ([Marketcalls](https://www.marketcalls.in/amibroker/vwap-intraday-trading-strategy-do-simple-trading-strategies-really-work-part4.html)). The papers from Andrew Aziz's research group are the authors' own working papers, not peer-reviewed. Their 5-minute QQQ ORB assumed zero slippage, and an independent replication breaks even at about 2.2 cents a share, "only about two spreads of execution cost" ([Brusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/qqq-opening-bias-5min)). Their 1,637% "stocks in play" result is net of commission only. It comes from a filter that picks the top 20 stocks by relative volume out of about 7,000 US stocks; the same ORB without the filter had a Sharpe ratio of 0.48 ([Tradoki](https://blog.tradoki.com/posts/opening-range-breakout-stocks-in-play-research); [Quantified Strategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/stocks-in-play-trading-strategy-day-trading/)). Their SPY intraday-momentum strategy fell from a Sharpe ratio of about 1.3 in the original test period to 0.39 after publication ([Swedroe](https://larryswedroe.substack.com/p/when-beat-the-market-meets-the-real); [Paz](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7290621)). One replication on index CFDs summed up the pattern: "The pattern survives but the trade does not" ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). For the same stop distance, Indian retail charges in R are roughly ten times what the US papers assumed, so the papers' stop of 10% of the daily range (ATR) would cost 0.5–0.8R per trade here.

The best-supported Indian intraday effect is that the first half-hour's return helps predict the last half-hour ([Motwani et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383567351_Hedging_Demand_and_Intraday_Momentum_within_the_Indian_Stock_Market)). It is an index-level effect that needs index derivatives to trade, and nobody has tested it with costs. In one simulation, a Nifty momentum rule's Sharpe ratio fell to zero at about 11 bps of cost per trade ([QuantInsti](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/transaction-costs-automated-trading/)). Nobody has tested Gujral & Vaidya's methods either: 20-period moving averages, floor pivots and the Central Pivot Range, and the first-hour "morning range" ([Vision Books](http://www.visionbooksindia.com/details.asp?isbn=9386268159); [Goodreads reviews](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40118580-how-to-make-money-in-intraday-trading)).

### One long-only delivery setup, held for weeks, should carry the real money

The primary track should be a single swing setup family, defined in advance from that evidence. Its universe is the F&O list: about 213 stocks that must pass SEBI's liquidity thresholds and trade without fixed price limits, so an order of ₹2,500–10,000 has negligible market impact ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/data-patterns-kvb-syrma-sgs-among-6-stocks-available-for-fo-trading-from-oct-28-561684-2026-10-15); [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-tweaks-criteria-for-inclusion-deletion-of-stocks-from-f-o-segment-124062701130_1.html)).

After each close, the tool ranks stocks by volatility-adjusted 6- and 12-month return, the way the NSE index does. It flags highly ranked stocks that close at or near their 52-week high, but only while the Nifty is above a long moving average. The trader enters by hand the next morning with a limit order priced to fill immediately, placed after the opening auction rather than at the auction price. The stop is wide enough, roughly 3–8%, to keep charges at 0.10–0.15R, and position size is ₹200 divided by the per-share stop distance. Exits use a time limit of a few weeks or a trailing stop, executed as a single sell order, because each extra day of selling adds another ₹15.34 DP charge. A 5% stop means a ₹4,000 position, so the account can hold about five positions at once without borrowing. Zerodha's margin trading facility (MTF) is uneconomic here, because its interest and pledge fees push a round trip to roughly 0.56–1.25R depending on position size and holding time ([Zerodha MTF FAQ](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/margins/margin-trading-facility/articles/margin-trading-facility-mtf-faqs)).

Every parameter above is a candidate to be fixed once on the setup card before replay, not a tested value. The evidence covers holding periods of one to six months, which is why holds of weeks fit it better than the few-day swings common in trading lore.

Intraday runs alongside as a paper-only track, because the trader believes judgement and the books matter, and only data can test that. It should use one or two setups that can be coded exactly. One is a 15-minute opening-range breakout limited to F&O stocks trading on unusually high volume that morning. Another is Gujral & Vaidya's first-hour range breakout or their pullback to the 20-period moving average. They should take only flags whose stop is at least 0.75–1% away, which keeps charges at 0.14R or less. They should stay far below SEBI's 500-trades-a-year danger zone and close every position before Zerodha's forced square-off. They move to real money only by passing the same statistical tests as swing, after charges and assumed slippage. F&O is excluded outright: it cannot be traded at ₹200 risk, has the worst base rates and highest costs, and cannot be backtested from Kite.

Expect the primary track to be unspectacular. Momentum went through a losing year to June 2026, and at index level its normal drawdowns are several times the trial's 10% stop. Overnight gaps are the main swing risk. A stock that opens 1% beyond a 5% stop loses 1.2R, and five similar positions gapping down together could cost five or six R in one session. Total open risk should therefore be capped, and the trend filter treated as risk control rather than a source of profit. Luck matters even with a real edge. A genuine +0.2R setup with 2R targets still ends 100 trades more than 10R below its starting balance 12% of the time. A setup with no edge before costs and 0.1R of costs still looks profitable after 100 trades 16–28% of the time ([simulations](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20plan/validation_sims/sims_output.txt)).

## At ₹20,000 the tool's fixed costs exceed any plausible edge

Because 1R is 1% of the account, every ₹200 of monthly cost equals 1R and 1% of capital. Take an edge at the sequential test's design level of +0.2R net per trade, which is already optimistic given that independent replications of intraday ORB find edges of only 0.07–0.13R before costs ([Brusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/qqq-opening-bias-5min); [MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). At 6–12 swing trades a month it earns 1.2–2.4R, which is **₹240–480 a month**. An edge the size of the index-level momentum premium, about 4.9 percentage points a year over the Nifty 200, is worth **roughly ₹1,000 a year** on ₹20,000 ([HDFC Mutual Fund](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf)).

Against that, the stack the trader specified has two fixed costs. Kite Connect costs ₹500 a month; the free Personal plan carries no live or historical market data ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/historical-data-and-live-market-data-payment-plan); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/for-traders-who-can-code-zerodha-cuts-rates-as-nithin-kamath-explains-nses-new-rulebook-474897-2025-05-06)). Claude Pro costs $20 a month ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)), about ₹1,760 at an assumed ₹88 to the dollar, before any GST. The "lean build" column below uses free daily data and pays per verdict through Anthropic's API instead.

|---|---|---|---|
| Trading charges, excluding slippage | ₹850–1,700 (20–40 trades at 0.5% stops) | ₹145–290 (6–12 trades at 5% stops) | ₹145–290 |
| Market data | Kite Connect ₹500 | Kite Connect ₹500 | ₹0 after a one-off ₹500 month to download history, then a free broker feed |

The arithmetic is blunt. Fixed costs stay flat while profit grows with capital. At 1% risk per trade, the capital needed to cover fixed costs is the monthly fixed cost divided by (1% × trades per month × edge per trade). At 10 trades a month and +0.2R, Kite plus Pro needs **about ₹1.13 lakh** of capital to break even, and Kite alone needs about ₹25,000. On ₹20,000, the specified stack loses money even with a real edge; the lean stack can roughly pay its way.

Two practical points follow. First, the tool's fixed costs belong in a separate research budget, outside both the trading capital and the ₹2,000 trial budget; Kite's fee alone would use up that budget in four months. Second, Zerodha's reported ₹500 Kite Connect credit for months with at least ₹2,000 of brokerage ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/free-kite-connect-subscription-for-active-users/198623)) does not help delivery trading, where brokerage is zero. Earning it would take about 100 intraday orders a month.

Free data exists for the lean build. Fyers' free tier streams 100 symbols and serves five years of daily history ([Fyers](https://fyers.in/api-docs/v3/getting-started/plans-and-limits/)), and Upstox's free API serves minute candles from January 2022 ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/developer/api-documentation/v3/get-historical-candle-data)). At Kite's three historical requests a second, one paid month is enough to download a decade of history ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/14806/historical-data-is-now-free)). Mixing sources means reconciling adjustments for splits and bonuses, because Kite serves only adjusted prices ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/16280/historical-5-minute-api-data-corporate-action-price-and-volume-adjustments)).

The honest benchmark is a fund, not zero. HDFC, Kotak, UTI, Motilal Oswal and Baroda BNP Paribas run passive Nifty200 Momentum 30 funds that deliver the evidence-backed premium at fund costs ([HDFC Mutual Fund](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf); [Motilal Oswal FAQ](https://www.motilaloswalmf.com/mf/assets/pdf/Motilal-Oswal-Nifty-200-Momentum-30-ETF-Index-Fund-FAQ.pdf)). If the only goal were to grow ₹20,000, a fund would beat any tool once the tool's fixed costs are counted. The tool earns its keep only by finding out, cheaply and before larger capital is at stake, whether the trader's setups and judgement add something a fund cannot. The scoreboard should therefore show the index fund as a benchmark line.

Tax narrows swing's advantage for a modest earner. Gains on shares held for delivery up to 12 months are short-term capital gains taxed at 20%. Long-term gains are taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year, and Budget 2026 left both rates unchanged ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/budget/news/budget-2026-ltcg-tax-rates-unchanged-for-fy27-fm-sitharaman-126020100424_1.html)). Intraday profit counts as speculative business income and F&O profit as non-speculative business income. Both are taxed at slab rates and reported on the ITR-3 return. Speculative losses can only offset speculative profits and carry forward for four years; F&O losses carry forward for eight. Either carry-forward requires filing the return on time ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/how-traders-are-taxed-in-india); [Income Tax Department, s.113](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-113-98); [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/set-off-losses-income-tax-act-2025-section-mapping)). Under the new regime, the section 87A rebate covers total income up to ₹12 lakh but excludes capital gains taxed at special rates ([Harun Raaj](https://www.harunraaj.com/blog/section-87a-rebate-new-regime-capital-gains)). A trader below that line could therefore owe nothing on intraday profit while still paying 20% on swing gains.

The Income-tax Act 2025, in force from 1 April 2026, renumbered the sections (speculative losses moved from s.73 to s.113) but changed none of these classifications. Turnover for tax-audit purposes is measured as absolute profits plus absolute losses, which at this scale sits far below any audit threshold ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/fo-derivative-turnover-calculation-tax-audit-section-44ab.html)). Advance tax applies only if the year's tax after TDS exceeds ₹10,000 ([JM Financial](https://www.jmfinancialservices.in/blogs-and-articles/advance-tax-deadline-fy-2026-27-who-is-exempt-and-who-should-pay)).

## An order-free tool sidesteps SEBI's algo rules, not Kite's or NSE's

SEBI's framework for retail algorithmic trading was issued on 4 February 2025 and has been fully in force since 1 April 2026, after two postponements. It is built around orders: API access needs a static IP address whitelisted by the broker, every order sent through an API carries an exchange-issued algo ID, and self-built strategies must be registered if they exceed 10 orders a second ([Mondaq](https://mondaq.com:443/india/commoditiesderivativesstock-exchanges/1581380/sebi-circular-safeguarding-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading); [SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2025/1759232056254.pdf)). NSE treats "all orders received via API from clients" as algo orders ([NSE FAQ](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_Retail%20Algo_03112025_NSE.pdf)). Zerodha requires the static IP only for placing orders through the API; the live-data WebSocket and other APIs work from any IP address ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/static-ip); [Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/15359/query-regarding-static-ip-requirement-for-kite-connect-api-data-only-usage)). A tool that streams data, paper-trades internally and leaves every order to the Kite app therefore sits outside the binding parts of the framework, even on an ordinary home internet connection. **The line not to cross is a single order sent through the API.** A "one-click execute" button would bring in the static-IP requirement, algo-ID tagging and the daily-logout rules. No official text addresses data-only API keys directly, so this conclusion rests on the framework's order-based definitions.

Kite Connect itself imposes a daily routine and a privacy rule. The access token expires at 6 AM the next day ([Kite Connect docs](https://kite.trade/docs/connect/v3/user/)), and Zerodha staff say automating the login, including the one-time password, "is against the exchange regulations" ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/45535)). The trader therefore logs in by hand once a day. The data is for personal use only: displaying or redistributing it breaches exchange data-vending policies ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/kite-connect-api-faqs)).

The technical limits shape the build. One API key allows three WebSocket connections of up to 3,000 instruments each ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/51532)), and ticks are snapshots of at most about one per second per instrument rather than every trade ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/43580/)). Only "full" mode carries exchange timestamps, and index ticks carry no volume ([kiteconnect 5.2.2](https://pypi.org/project/kiteconnect/5.2.2/)). Historical prices are adjusted for splits, bonuses and large dividends, with no unadjusted series ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/16280/historical-5-minute-api-data-corporate-action-price-and-volume-adjustments)). Delisted stocks are not served at all, so any replay run on today's stock lists inherits survivorship bias: it silently leaves out the companies that failed.

NSE's trading rules changed repeatedly in 2026, and the tool must encode them by date and by type of stock:

Because the official close of F&O stocks is now an auction price, any pivot or price level built on "the close" must use one consistent definition.

Price limits and short-selling rules are the other traps. Non-F&O stocks have fixed daily price bands of 2–20%. A stock that hits its band can lock there, leaving a stop order unable to execute. F&O stocks instead have a 10% operating range that widens in steps after cooling-off pauses ([NSE](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-market-price-bands); [Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explainer-on-new-price-band-rules-for-stocks-trading-in-the-fo-segment)), which is another reason to keep the universe to F&O names. Retail traders may short sell only within the day, and naked short selling is banned. If an intraday short gets trapped because the stock is locked at its upper price limit, it becomes a short delivery. The exchange then closes it out at up to 20% above the auction-day close ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-allows-short-selling-by-all-investors-ban-on-naked-short-selling-continues/73065.html); [Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/trending/consequences-of-short-delivery-nse-bse)), a loss far beyond 1R. The tool should therefore suppress intraday short flags near the upper band. SEBI's margin rules cap intraday equity leverage at 5× and give no extra leverage on F&O ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/margin-leverage-and-product-and-order-types/articles/how-much-margins-leverage-does-zerodha-provide)). On ₹20,000, the tightest stop that can still carry a full ₹200 risk is therefore 0.2%.

Keeping the tool private keeps the regulatory picture simple. Using Claude privately for one's own decisions falls outside SEBI's rules for research analysts, investment advisers and AI, which apply to regulated firms. Posting named-stock flags or take/skip verdicts to a group or channel, especially for any payment, counts as research or advice that needs SEBI registration, and registered analysts must disclose their use of AI ([Compliance Calendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/sebi-research-analyst-third-amendment-regulations-2024)). Since 1 July 2026, even content labelled "educational" may only use price data at least 30 days old ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/sebi-revises-norms-for-sharing-and-usage-of-price-data-for-educational-purposes)). Book passages kept for private research likely fall within the "fair dealing" exception for private use in s.52(1)(a) of the Copyright Act ([BananaIP](https://bananaip.com/?p=39294)). Stripping the copy protection from an e-book, however, risks the anti-circumvention offence in s.65A, where the law is unsettled ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/columns/piracy-of-the-public-domain-a-right-without-a-remedy-in-indian-law)). The safe course is short excerpts typed from the physical copy, kept out of any public code repository.

## A sequential test, not 100 trades, decides when a setup earns money

A hundred trades cannot tell a small edge from luck. Write σ for the spread (standard deviation) of each trade's result in R, and δ for the true edge per trade. The number of trades needed to be 95% confident the edge is positive, with an 80% chance of detecting it, is about 6.18 × (σ/δ)². At σ of 1.0–1.5R, that is **70–160 trades for a +0.3R edge, 155–350 for +0.2R, and 620–1,400 for +0.1R**. After 100 trades, the average result has a standard error of 0.10–0.15R, so its 95% range spans about ±0.2–0.3R. A real +0.1R edge is detected only 16–26% of the time, and the same genuine edge loses money over 100 trades about one time in five ([simulations](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20plan/validation_sims/sims_output.txt)).

The current pass mark is therefore not a real test. It requires at least 100 trades and three months, a profit after costs, and losses kept within ₹2,000. For a setup with no edge, its chance of passing by luck ranges from 0.3% to 32%, depending on the payoff shape and costs. With ten no-edge setups on the scoreboard, the chance that at least one passes reaches 45–79% ([simulations](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20plan/validation_sims/sims_output.txt)). Higher profit targets make this worse because they widen the spread of results. A 2R target gives σ near 1.47R, and a 1.2R target near 1.1R, so the same edge needs about 1.8 times as many trades to confirm.

How the ₹2,000 stop is measured matters more than any other choice. Measured from the account's peak, a 10R limit ends a genuine +0.2R setup with 2R targets in **52% of 100-trade runs and 80% of 200-trade runs**. Measured as a loss from the starting balance, it ends only 12–14% of them ([simulations](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20plan/validation_sims/sims_output.txt)). The limit should therefore mean equity falling to ₹18,000, not a 10R fall from a high. If the trader prefers to measure from the peak, risk per trade should drop to ₹100, so that the same ₹2,000 equals 20R. During paper trading no money is at risk, so the ₹2,000 rule should give way to the sequential test's fail line. That line works like a floor starting near −₹1,900 to −₹2,400 and rising ₹20 per trade.

Before any data is touched, each setup gets a locked pre-registration. It records the rules, parameters, stock universe, timeframe, the assumptions for fills, slippage and costs, the main metric, and the pass and fail thresholds. The main metric is mean net R per trade, with its uncertainty calculated by grouping trades from the same day, because those trades move together. Next comes replay, which tradingtool's design decision record already reserves for testing setups alone ([ADR 0001](/home/user/tradingtool/docs/adr/0001-setups-are-code-model-judges-trader-decides.md)).

The history is split in time. The earliest two-thirds is used to develop the setup, and the latest third is held back and opened only once, for a final test. For daily swing setups, 10–15 years of data leaves a held-back test period of several years. Every variant tried on any data adds to a counter, K, because searching through variants manufactures apparent edges. With σ of 1.25R and 300 trades per variant, the best of 10 variants that truly have no edge is expected to show +0.11R ([simulations](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20plan/validation_sims/sims_output.txt)). When 7,846 technical rules were tested on a century of Dow Jones data, the best rule's apparent edge did not hold up in the following decade ([Sullivan, Timmermann & White](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00163)).

The replay passes only if every one of these conditions holds:

| Replay test | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Sample size | at least 200 trades in the held-back test period |
| Significance in the held-back period | mean net R above zero, with a t-statistic (using day-grouped errors) of at least 1.645 for one candidate setup, 1.96 for two, 2.13 for three |
| Correction for the variants tried | Deflated Sharpe Ratio of at least 0.95, with N set to K ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094)) |
| Robustness | edge stays positive with costs raised 50% and entry delayed by one bar |
| Fit with the ₹2,000 budget | resampling the trade list shows at most about a 20% chance of falling 10R below the start within 200 trades; otherwise halve risk per trade |

If several setups or scoreboard lines are compared, use White's Reality Check or Hansen's SPA test on daily R totals, which correct for the comparison itself ([White 2000](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152); [Hansen 2005](https://doi.org/10.1198/073500105000000063)). A useful cap on the number of variants is K ≈ exp(Tδ²/8σ²), where T is the number of trades. That allows about 24 variants at 1,000 trades and δ = 0.2R, but about 600 at 2,000 trades ([verification run](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20plan/validation_sims/verification_checks_output.txt)), one more advantage of long daily histories. Setups taken from books deserve a stricter t-statistic of 2.5–3, because their authors likely chose them for working on past charts. The same reasoning lies behind Harvey, Liu and Zhu's recommended hurdle of t > 3 for new findings ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059)).

| Spread of each trade's result (σ) | PASS when cumulative P&L reaches | FAIL when cumulative P&L falls to | Mean R needed to pass at 100 / 200 / 300 trades |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0R | ₹2,773 + ₹20 × trades | −₹1,558 + ₹20 × trades | 0.239 / 0.169 / 0.146R |
| 1.1R | ₹3,355 + ₹20 × trades | −₹1,885 + ₹20 × trades | 0.268 / 0.184 / 0.156R |
| 1.25R | ₹4,332 + ₹20 × trades | −₹2,435 + ₹20 × trades | 0.317 / 0.208 / 0.172R |
| 1.47R | ₹5,991 + ₹20 × trades | −₹3,367 + ₹20 × trades | 0.400 / 0.250 / 0.200R |

Forward paper trading then runs a sequential probability ratio test (SPRT) on the net R of every flag the frozen setup raises ([Wald 1945](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731118)). The test checks after every trade whether the evidence better fits no edge or a +0.2R edge, aiming for at most a 5% chance of passing a no-edge setup and a 20% chance of failing a real one. In formula terms, with n trades and cumulative result ΣR, a setup passes when ΣR ≥ 13.86σ² + 0.1n and fails when ΣR ≤ −7.79σ² + 0.1n. The table above gives these lines in rupees. σ comes from the replay. A pass needs at least 50 trades and three calendar months, and the verdict becomes "not proven" at 300 trades.

The rising fail line has a counterintuitive consequence worth internalizing. At σ = 1.25R, a setup that is ₹1,500 up after 200 trades **fails**, because an average of 0.04R per trade is far more consistent with no edge than with +0.2R. In simulation, the test passes a no-edge setup only 3–4% of the time, passes a genuine +0.2R edge 62–77% of the time, and reaches a decision in 85–185 trades on average ([simulations](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20plan/validation_sims/sims2_output.txt)).

A pass is not proof. Suppose one candidate setup in ten truly has a +0.2R edge; then roughly seven in ten passes are real. If only one in twenty does, about half are. That illustrative calculation, from the error rates above, argues for running few setups. It also argues for requiring the paper results to agree with the replay. Flag a mismatch when z = (paper mean − replay mean) ÷ √(σ²/n_paper + σ²/n_replay) falls below −2, which usually means the replay was too optimistic about fills, costs or lookahead. When several setups run at once, the pass line must rise to compensate: for three setups, the 13.86σ² intercept rises by about 40%, to about 19.4σ².

Only a setup that passes goes live, at ₹100–200 risk. Orders are typed into Kite by hand, real fills are logged against the paper fills, the same pass and fail lines are recomputed from live results, and the ₹2,000 limit is measured from the starting balance. Position size grows only while live results stay consistent with paper.

Judging the model's verdicts and the trader's final calls needs far larger samples. Those are subsets of the same flags, so they must be judged as taken versus skipped, not against all flags. At a 50% take rate, detecting a 0.3R gap between taken and skipped flags needs about **430 flags**, and a 0.5R gap about 155. Comparing the model with the trader uses only the flags where they disagree ([simulations](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20plan/validation_sims/sims_output.txt)).

Paper trading is not limited by capital, so every flag across the universe counts toward the setup's test, even when the real account could hold only five positions. At about one flag a session, the sequential test typically decides within four to nine months, while a verdict on the model filter takes well over a year. The three-month minimum guards against testing in a single market regime, not against statistical noise; coverage of different regimes comes mainly from the replay.

## Claude's verdicts can be scored forward but never backtested

No study tests the design tradingtool proposes: a model judging rule-based flags for a human who makes the decision. The nearest evidence is mixed. GPT-4's scores of news headlines predicted next-day US returns, mainly in small stocks and after bad news, but the strategy's returns fell as LLM use spread ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v6)). A live test of GPT-4 stock ratings in 2023 found positive returns, but its authors advised treating models as assistants rather than final judges ([Pelster & Val](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v59y2024ics1544612323011583.html)). Tests of LLM trading agents on data the models could not have seen, or live with real money, are less flattering. In StockBench, no agent consistently beat buy-and-hold, and the agents failed to beat it during a market downturn ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209v1)). A broader backtest found that reported LLM advantages "deteriorate significantly", with models too cautious in bull markets and too aggressive in bear markets ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v3)). In a real-money contest, four of six models lost money, one reportedly by 62.7% ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). There is no Indian evidence and no published trading evaluation of any Claude 5.x model. The most plausible value is consistency rather than prediction: catching results days, gaps, nearness to price limits, bad news and rule violations. That is a hypothesis for the scoreboard to test, not an established finding.

The verdicts cannot be backtested at all. Opus, Sonnet and Haiku 5.5 all have a **June 2026 training cutoff** ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)), so for earlier dates they may already know how the market moved. Research shows that telling a model to ignore later information does not remove this lookahead ([Sarkar & Vafa](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f205530/f205530.pdf)). Hiding company names and dates also fails, because models can work them out from small clues ([Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)). Replaying verdicts on candles from before July 2026 therefore tests the software, not the edge. That is exactly why ADR 0001 limits the scoring of verdicts to forward paper trading.

The forward protocol is strict. At each flag, the tool assembles everything the model will see: candles up to the flag, rule checks computed in Python, timestamped headlines and passage IDs. It stores a hash of that bundle before the call and never asks again once the outcome is known. The model version, effort level and prompt version stay fixed within each evaluation block, and web tools are disabled so that no unlogged information enters. Frozen bundles from after the cutoff can be re-run later to measure randomness without any leakage.

Each known failure mode needs a countermeasure in the design:

| Failure mode | Evidence | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Answers vary from run to run, and randomness cannot be turned off | any temperature setting returns an error from API version 2026-10-21, and thinking is always on for Opus 5.5 ([Anthropic migration guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/migration-2026-10-21); [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)) | re-run 10–20% of stored bundles three to five times and report how often "take" flips to "skip" |
| Stated confidence is overconfident | agreement across repeated samples is a better signal ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063v2)) | ask for a probability that the target is hit before the stop, score it with the Brier score, and never let confidence change position size |
| Models drift toward the user's framing | phrasing a request as a question reduced this more than instructing against it ([UK AISI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23971)) | ask whether the flag should be taken; require reasons against before reasons for; offer an explicit "abstain"; supply the setup's success rate from replay |
| Citations without grounding are often invented | 18% of GPT-4's citations in one study ([Walters & Wilder](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5.pdf)) | give passages IDs; each cited reason must quote at most 40 words verbatim; Python rejects any quote not found in the supplied text |
| Failed calls | a run can time out, error, or end without the expected JSON | log "no verdict" and never default to "take"; make a fresh call for each flag so earlier verdicts cannot anchor later ones |

A shadow verdict from the cheaper Haiku model, run on the identical bundle and hidden until after the final call, costs almost nothing and shows where the models disagree. Its value is judged on those disagreements, using a paired test suited to that comparison ([Dietterich](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~tgd/publications/dietterich-approximate-statistical-tests-nc1998.pdf)). The following invocation, run from an empty working directory, uses only documented flags, although this exact combination is untested:

```
claude -p --model claude-opus-5-5 --effort low \
  --system-prompt-file verdict_v1.txt --tools "" --disallowedTools "mcp__*" \
  --strict-mcp-config --disable-slash-commands --no-session-persistence \
  --max-turns 2 --output-format json --json-schema "$(cat verdict_schema_v1.json)" \
  < payload.txt
```

Running this through `claude -p` on Claude Pro works technically. Opus 5.5 is Pro's default model in Claude Code, and the command line returns JSON checked against the schema in a `structured_output` field, which is missing when a run fails ([Claude Code model configuration](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config); [Claude Code headless docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless)). Pinned model IDs matter, because plain names like `opus` switch to newer models when they ship ([Claude Code model configuration](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config)). Four problems come with the Pro route. Pro's five-hour and weekly allowances are shared with all other Claude use and have no published numbers ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11145838-using-claude-code-with-your-pro-or-max-plan)). The fast `--bare` mode never reads subscription logins and "will become the default for `-p` in a future release", so scripts using the subscription may break on an update unless the Claude Code version is pinned ([Claude Code headless docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless)). Latency is undocumented and must be measured. Most important, Claude Code on Pro falls under the Consumer Terms ([Claude Code legal](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)), whose list of prohibited uses includes "To rely upon the Services, the Materials, or the Actions to buy or sell securities" ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)).

The API's Commercial Terms have no such clause ([Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). However, the Usage Policy taking effect on 12 November 2026 classes personalized buy or sell recommendations as high-risk. It requires meaningful review by a qualified person and disclosure of AI use, with no explicit exception for someone deciding for themselves ([Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). Paper trading involves no purchase or sale, so using Pro during the trial is defensible. Before any verdict informs a real order, the verdict runner should switch to an API key, either through the Python SDK or `claude -p --bare` with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, and the trader should confirm the position with Anthropic. This is not legal advice.

The API is also cheaper at swing volumes. Opus 5.5 costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens; Haiku 5.5 costs $0.10 and $0.50 ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). A verdict of 5,000–15,000 tokens therefore costs about $0.03–0.10 on Opus and under $0.003 on Haiku. Opus plus a Haiku shadow at five flags a day comes to $5–9 a month. Measured against the account, Pro costs 8.8R a month. At about ten taken trades a month, it would need to improve each trade by nearly 0.9R to pay for itself, and no study suggests a filter that strong. Unless Pro is already paid for other reasons, the verdict layer should start on the API with Haiku and add Opus as the main verdict once the flag rate is known. In its first year it should be treated as an auditor that writes down reasons, not as a forecaster that sets position size.

## Six build phases put evidence ahead of plumbing

tradingtool today is a 389-line starting point. It has a glossary, one design decision record (ADR) that fixes the flow, a news reader and a project setup ([CONTEXT.md](/home/user/tradingtool/CONTEXT.md); [ADR 0001](/home/user/tradingtool/docs/adr/0001-setups-are-code-model-judges-trader-decides.md); [news.py](/home/user/tradingtool/src/tradingtool/news.py)). The ADR's flow is: code raises flags, the model gives a verdict, the trader makes the final call after seeing it, and every flag is paper traded for a three-line scoreboard. The news reader uses only Python's standard library to read NSE corporate announcements, Economic Times and Google News RSS feeds, and has seven tests. The project is set up for Python 3.13 under uv, with no dependencies. It describes itself as a "personal intraday tool" ([pyproject.toml](/home/user/tradingtool/pyproject.toml)). It leaves open the candle size and how flags are timestamped, the paper-trade entry price, storage, a name for the hidden Haiku verdict, how to handle flags with no verdict or no final call, the trial metrics, and any holding period other than intraday.

Four design rules follow from the ADR. Setups must be pure functions of candles, never reading the computer's clock. Storage must be append-only, with nothing deleted or merged. The final call cannot be entered before the verdict is shown. Every verdict row must record the model, a hash of the prompt and the timings.

The core is one function, `on_candle(series, candle) → flags`, plus a paper-trading engine driven by candles. Both are fed the same way from stored history in replay and from live data. Swing setups use Kite's official daily candles in an evening job. Intraday setups use 1-minute candles built from live ticks, rolled up into 5- and 15-minute candles starting at 09:15. Each candle closes at its boundary, and a flag is stamped at the close of the candle that completes it.

The paper engine follows fixed, conservative rules. It enters at the open of the next candle plus slippage, assumes the stop was hit first when one candle touches both stop and target, and fills at the open when price gaps past the stop. It sizes each trade as floor(₹200 ÷ distance from entry to stop), and records the flag as "unfillable" rather than dropping it when that rounds to zero. It applies a versioned cost table to each leg and reports R = (P&L − costs) ÷ ₹200.

Storage is SQLite in WAL mode. Experiments during the research showed a live DuckDB writer locking out a second process even in read-only mode, while SQLite readers were never blocked ([DuckDB 2.0.0](https://pypi.org/project/duckdb/2.0.0/); [Python sqlite3](https://docs.python.org/3/library/sqlite3.html)). Tables for candles, flags, verdicts, final calls, paper-trade events and runs are made append-only with database triggers that abort any UPDATE or DELETE. If the tool starts late, it replays the morning's candles and records any flags as "backfill" flags that are paper traded, rather than silently suppressing them.

edge-scanner is MIT-licensed and built for US markets, but a handful of its patterns are worth adapting with attribution ([LICENSE](../LICENSE)). Its quote-to-bar builder derives volume from changes in cumulative day volume, treats a fall below 20% of the previous total as a reset and smaller drops as corrections, and corrects bar highs and lows whenever the day's high or low moves ([quote_bars.py](../scanner/data/quote_bars.py)). It assigns ticks to minutes by the computer's clock (line 182), however, and for Kite that must become the exchange timestamp converted explicitly to IST, because pykiteconnect returns local-time datetimes with no time zone ([kiteconnect 5.2.2](https://pypi.org/project/kiteconnect/5.2.2/)). Its "once" and "edge" latches, which stop a trigger repeating and are scoped per configuration and primed at startup, carry a hard-won lesson: skipping latch updates outside market hours once produced 200 false "EMA crossed above" alerts at the open instead of 52 ([custom_setups.py](../scanner/custom_setups.py)). Its split between conditions fixed for the session and conditions checked on every bar, failing closed when data is missing, maps onto the morning "in play" test and intraday filters ([conditions.py](../scanner/conditions.py)). Its feed-comparison rule, "Ready when every overlap is >= 97%", becomes a nightly check that live and historical candles agree ([check_schwab_match.py](../scripts/check_schwab_match.py)).

Three things must not be copied. The alert sink's five-minute cooldown and 5,000-alert trim would discard flags, and the alert store deletes files older than five days ([alert_sink.py](../scanner/alert_sink.py); [alert_store.py](../scanner/alert_store.py)). Its rule for completing a candle waits for the next candle's first bar, which would leave NSE's last candle of the day open until the next morning ([trigger_catalog.py](../scanner/trigger_catalog.py)). US assumptions must also be replaced. New York times become IST, US trading hours become 09:15–15:30, and the 04:00 premarket becomes the pre-open auction price. SPY as the market gauge becomes the NIFTY 50, whose ticks carry no volume, so an index VWAP cannot be built from ticks. US sector ETFs become NSE sector indices, and US round-lot rules are dropped.

Two daily routines result. The swing routine runs after the close. The trader logs in to Kite once: a `tradingtool login` command opens the login page, and a small web listener on the Mac (127.0.0.1) catches the token Kite sends back. The tool refuses tokens issued before 06:00 IST that day. Whether Kite accepts a localhost redirect still needs checking. The tool then fetches the day's candles, raises flags and gathers verdicts, and the trader reviews them in the terminal interface and records final calls. Orders go in by hand the next morning. The trader should confirm in Kite how to keep a standing stop order on a delivery holding, a detail the research could not verify.

The intraday routine checks the holiday calendar and the login token at 08:45. NSE's remaining 2026 weekday holidays are 10 and 24 November ([pandas-market-calendars](https://pypi.org/project/pandas-market-calendars/5.5.0/)). It captures the pre-open price after 09:12, once the auction price is fixed, and exits paper positions before 15:12 or 15:25, depending on the stock and the date. It closes out the day's candles at 15:30 and writes the live-versus-history comparison report at about 15:35.

Three tests protect the evidence. Flags computed on history cut off at any point must equal the start of the flags computed on full history, which catches lookahead. A fake `claude` program that returns canned JSON, leaves out the structured output, fails or hangs exercises every verdict status. Textual's headless test pilot confirms that the take and skip keys stay disabled until a verdict appears ([Textual 8.2.8](https://pypi.org/project/textual/8.2.8/)). Desktop notifications through desktop-notifier need a signed Python build, which python.org provides and Homebrew does not ([desktop-notifier](https://pypi.org/project/desktop-notifier/)). External backtesting frameworks should be avoided, because they would break the single shared code path for replay and live, and several carry AGPL or Commons Clause licences.

Four sets of materials have to come from the trader.

First, the books themselves: Gujral & Vaidya's *How to Make Money in Intraday Trading* (Vision Books, 2018, ISBN 9789386268150) and Aziz's *How to Day Trade for a Living* and *Advanced Techniques in Day Trading* ([Vision Books](http://www.visionbooksindia.com/details.asp?isbn=9386268159); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/60679643-how-to-day-trade-for-a-living); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/57760551)). The research could reach only summaries, so the exact rules must be read from the pages: entry triggers, stops, targets, timeframes, the definition of "exceptional candles", the four trend-entry tactics, the gap rules and the money-management rules.

Second, sources for the swing setup cards, which the trader's intraday books do not cover. NSE's Nifty200 Momentum 30 methodology ([NSE Indices whitepaper](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/nifty200_momentum_30_index_whitepaper_sep_20.pdf)) and Raju's 52-week-high study ([SSRN 4587697 copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/694152012/SSRN-id4587697)) can serve as the "book passages" a verdict cites.

Third, for every setup, three to eight passages of 100–300 words. Each needs the book, edition, chapter and page and a stable ID such as AZ-ORB-03, and should be typed from the physical copy or exported from e-reader highlights. Each setup also needs a signed setup card stating the candle size, entry, stop, target, time exit, filters, and every gap the book leaves open.

Fourth, accounts and housekeeping. These are a Kite Connect app with its key, secret and redirect URL; optionally a free Fyers or Upstox account for data; and an Anthropic API key for the live stage. The Mac needs a python.org Python build and must stay awake through the session. The trader's income level also matters, because it decides whether the 87A rebate makes intraday profits effectively tax-free while swing gains pay 20%.

## Conclusion

The deciding factor for this trader is not which setup to trade but how large fixed costs are relative to capital. At ₹20,000, a tool costing 11% of the account each month cannot be justified by profits, only by what it reveals, and what it can reveal is genuinely scarce. No Indian regulator or academic has measured three things: swing-trading results net of current delivery costs, slippage on manually entered NSE orders, or whether a language model's take/skip judgement adds anything to rule-based flags. With pre-registration and a sequential test, even a failed trial is a result. A pass is the first point at which putting more money behind the method becomes defensible. The design choices that matter most are therefore about method: one code path, every flag paper traded, every variant counted, and every verdict's inputs hashed before the outcome exists.

Two dates will reshape the build within weeks: Anthropic's Usage Policy takes effect on 12 November 2026, and NSE returns to continuous trading until 15:30 on 7 December 2026. A third change, making `--bare` the default for `claude -p`, could arrive with any Claude Code update. A tool that stores its session rules, cost tables and verdict backend as versioned data can treat each such change as the start of a new evaluation block. A tool that hard-codes them will silently corrupt its own evidence. That argues for building the swing track and the verdict audit trail first. The intraday machinery is the part most exposed to these changes and least supported by evidence, so it can wait until the evening routine is producing numbers.
